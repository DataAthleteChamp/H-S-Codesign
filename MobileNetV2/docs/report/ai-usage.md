# AI Usage Statement

AI assistance was used during this project as an engineering support tool, not as the project decision-maker. It helped generate the consolidation plan for the repository, and that plan was then critiqued by a separate AI-assisted rubber-duck review. The review identified five blocking methodology issues at a high level: test-set contamination from augmented test images, validation/test leakage, a firmware preprocessing mismatch between `[0,1]` and `[-1,1]`, incorrect pairing assumptions for McNemar tests, and the risk of post-hoc scorecard p-hacking. These findings were accepted only after human review because they were technically valid and improved the report methodology.

AI was also used for code-review and refactoring suggestions, statistical-methodology recommendations, generation of evaluation-harness code, plot-styling assistance, and documentation scaffolding. For example, the team adopted exact McNemar testing rather than relying only on an asymptotic chi-square approximation because the effective originals-only test set is small and paired. This disclosure paragraph itself was also drafted with AI assistance and should be reviewed by the team before final submission.

AI was not used to make final design decisions, in accordance with the course rule. The final winning branch/model choice is signed off by the human team in `docs/decision.md`. Architecture choices such as MobileNetV2 depth multiplier, image size, quantization strategy, camera configuration, tensor-arena sizing, memory layout, and firmware behavior remain human engineering decisions; AI-generated experiments and summaries only informed those decisions. Dataset collection and labeling were performed by the team, not by AI. Interpretation of DTU 02214 course theory in the final report is likewise the team's responsibility.

The AI tools used are described at the level of GPT-class and Claude-class models. Every AI suggestion was reviewed by at least one human team member before being committed or included in the report, and AI did not have direct write access to `main`.
